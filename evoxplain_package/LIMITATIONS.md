# Limitations

Limitations of the **results**. For limitations of the code, see the
"Known issues" section of the README.

Most of what follows was found by checking our own claims. Several entries
correct earlier versions of this analysis, and the corrections are recorded.

---

## Geometry

**The regimes are not discrete clusters.** For elastic-net logistic regression
the explanation vectors lie on a continuous regularisation path. What the
analysis identifies are coherent regions at either end of that path with a
transition between them, not separated clouds with an empty region in between.
Clustering-based framing (k-means, silhouette-thresholded k*) overstates the
geometry: silhouette values are low and increase monotonically with k, which is
what happens when k-means segments a connected curve. Reported k* is also
unstable across lens and resampling. The claims in this package are stated at
the level of regimes along a path, and do not depend on discreteness.

**SHAP's third regime is the transition.** Where SHAP returns k*=3 against
LIME's k*=2, the additional segment brackets the transition window rather than
constituting a third endpoint. This is consistent with the continuous-path
reading and inconsistent with discrete attractors.

**The transition is one grid point wide, not zero.** In 99 of 100 splits regime
membership is a single threshold in C. The exception (split 815) has one run at
C=0.266 assigned across the boundary, inside the same 0.23-0.35 window where
every other split transitions. The accurate statement is that membership is
monotone in C up to at most one run within the transition window.

## Enrichment reporting

**Term counts overstate the evidence.** Seven ontology terms in these results
are generated by two adjacent paralogs, ACSM2B and ACSM5: four GO:MF CoA-ligase
terms (benzoate, decanoate, medium-chain fatty acid, fatty-acyl-CoA synthase)
and three REAC conjugation terms (amino acid conjugation, conjugation of
carboxylic acids, conjugation of salicylate with glycine), each with
`intersection_size = 2`. Describe this signal by its genes, not by its term
count.

**The "bitter taste" terms are an annotation artefact.** GO:0001580 and related
taste terms recur at 45-54% in one regime, but the genes driving them are
secreted cystatins (CST1, CST4), which are cysteine protease inhibitors and
well-documented tumour markers with no functional relationship to taste
perception. A third gene in the same term (ENSG00000162896) moves in the
*opposite* tumour-versus-normal direction from CST1/CST4, confirming the term
groups genes without a shared biology. These genes should be reported as
secreted cystatins; the taste label should not be used.

**An earlier cutoff made shared terms look regime-exclusive.** A first pass
retained only the top 3 terms per split-regime. Under that cutoff the
CoA-ligase terms appeared only in one regime. With 25 terms retained they
appear in both (28% and 37% of splits), and are part of the shared trunk rather
than a branch. Any exclusivity claim is contingent on the retention depth;
`top_terms_per_basin` is now recorded in the output files.

**Enrichment depends on the g:Profiler database release.** Results here were
produced against `gprofiler-official` 1.0.0 and database release
`e114_eg62_p19_27110d83`. Annotations change between releases; recurrence
figures are not reproducible without matching both.

**Background choice changes the Lewis result substantially.** Against
g:Profiler's default genome-wide domain, the 20 refits appeared to share a
neural theme (top term 7/20). Against the 22,835 measured genes as an explicit
background, that theme disappears: 10 of 20 models yield zero significant terms
and top recurrence falls to 2/20. The custom-background result is the correct
one; the earlier figure is recorded here because it is the number a default
analysis would produce.

## Scope of the TCGA result

**The dilution magnitude is grid-dependent.** The practitioner simulation uses
a 10-point log-spaced grid over C in [0.01, 10]. The regime boundary at
C ~ 0.23-0.35 falls near the middle of that grid, so roughly half of each
simulated practitioner's fits land in each regime by construction. This is a
defensible grid -- it is narrower than `LogisticRegressionCV`'s default -- but
the reported dilution is a property of that grid, not a general constant. A
grid confined to one regime would show no dilution. A grid-family sweep
quantifying dilution as a function of span has not been run.

**Practitioners receive the nearest available run, not the exact grid point.**
The simulation indexes into precomputed fits by nearest log-C neighbour, and
allows duplicates where a split's coverage is sparse near a target C.

**The regime boundary moves by about one grid step between splits.** A
practitioner selecting C near 0.25 could land in either regime depending on
which patients they drew. This is stated as an observation, not as a measured
selection-flip rate.

**The selection-flip rate is not measured.** The results here concern sweeping
and aggregating across a grid. They do not establish how often a realistic
model-selection procedure (nested CV, min-loss or 1-SE rule) lands in a
different regime on retraining. That is the number the deployment argument
requires and it has not been estimated.

**The keratinization branch skews squamous.** The keratin genes separate
tumour from normal across most tissue sites, but their strongest effects are in
lung squamous and colon. The lipid branch does not show a comparable skew.
Sample composition is ruled out as the *cause* of the fork by the
within-split argument, but the branch's tissue flavour is real.

**PCK1 conflicts with the reviewed literature outside gluconeogenic organs.**
PCK1 is reported as anti-oncogenic in liver and kidney but tumour-promoting in
cancers of non-gluconeogenic organs. In these data it is down in tumour in 21
of 30 sites, including bladder, colon and breast, where the literature predicts
up. Either the transcript-level pan-cancer picture differs from the functional
picture, or this effect is partly composition-driven in those tissues. HMGCS2,
down in 24 of 30 including non-gluconeogenic tissues with independent IHC
support, is the better-supported member of that branch.

**One gene symbol is unverified.** ENSG00000162896 is marked `?` in
`tissue_composition_check.py` and should be confirmed before it appears in any
written claim.

**Results are LIME-only unless stated.** The regime enrichment, phantom
simulation and branch analysis were run for the LIME lens. The SHAP counterpart
has not been completed.

## Controls

**The gradient-boosted control is degenerate and must not be cited.** Within
each split, all 100 XGB runs have identical accuracy, identical attribution
vectors (pairwise cosine 1.000000) and a single unique C value. k*=1 there is
arithmetic, not evidence that boosted trees resist explanation multiplicity. A
proper control with varied seeds and subsampling has not been run. A separate
XGB null on German Credit is uninformative for the same reason, compounded by
that dataset's small size.

**The fixed-C logistic-regression control is trivially k=1.** A convex problem
at a single hyperparameter has one optimum. It does not test whether admissible
variation converges.

**No control demonstrates convergence under genuine admissible variation.**
This is the main gap in the falsification battery: there is no case where real
variation across seeds or resamples is shown to produce a single regime.

## Lewis & Kemp audit

**The reproduction does not reach the published accuracy.** AUROC 0.813 mean
against 0.85 reported. The gap reflects preprocessing details not recoverable
from the public record. A second, independent reconstruction (Xena/TOIL, 885
samples, 40,950 genes) shows the same directionlessness, so the finding is not
an artefact of either pipeline.

**Anti-correlation does not rule out collinearity.** Genes whose attribution
sign flips across refits carry stronger marginal associations with the outcome
than stable genes. This was previously described as evidence against a
suppressor or collinearity explanation. It is not: strong-marginal genes
reversing sign in the presence of correlated predictors is the textbook
signature of collinearity, and it occurs for strong features as readily as weak
ones. The instability is real; its attribution to a cause is not settled.

**The shrinkage-covariance check is uninformative.** Computing similarity under
an empirical gene-gene covariance at p = 22,835, n = 915 is too unstable to
support a directional claim. The observed shift (anti-correlated pairs
0.49 -> 0.51) should be read as no evidence either way, not as evidence that
co-expression sharpens the divergence.

**Convergence at large list sizes is not agreement.** At 500 genes per model,
Jaccard rises to 0.577 and every term recurs in 20/20 models -- but the
recurring terms are broad housekeeping sets (histone deacetylase activity,
RNA Pol II pre-initiation), and the averaged panel's appearance rate in
individual models *falls* from 7.3/20 to 3.4/20 over the same range. Rising
overlap with falling panel correspondence indicates overlap by volume. The null
at publication-realistic sizes is the finding.

## What averaging does and does not do

Aggregation subtracts and displaces. It was not observed to fabricate — but this was tested only at the level of ontology terms. In the TCGA practitioner simulation, every term in every aggregated report traces back to a term present in at least one individual regime; no term appeared that no model supported. This does not extend to genes. The Lewis averaged panel's genes appear in a mean of 7.3 of 20 refits, and 15 of its 50 genes appear in 5 or fewer — so the published-style panel does contain genes that most equally accurate models do not select, assembled by summation rather than by any model's reasoning. The corresponding term-level check on the Lewis panel is uninformative, because that panel yields no significant terms at all against the measured-gene background. Whether aggregation can manufacture a term that no accurate model supports remains untested in the dispersed regime, and unknown in general.
